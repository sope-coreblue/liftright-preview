This is an equipment inspection. It is not operator training, operator evaluation or licensing. We inspect the truck, not the driver.

Plain terms

Forklift / lift truck
Any powered industrial truck that lifts a load on forks or an attachment. Counterbalance forklifts are the most common type.
Reach truck / order picker
Narrow-aisle trucks used in racking. Inspected under the same programme with attention to their masts and operator platforms.
Attachment
Clamps, rotators, side-shifters and similar devices fitted to the carriage. They change capacity and are checked with the truck.

Equipment we inspect

  • Counterbalance forklifts: electric, LPG and diesel
  • Reach trucks and narrow-aisle trucks
  • Order pickers and stock pickers
  • Powered pallet trucks and walkie stackers
  • Fitted attachments such as side-shifters, clamps and rotators

Tell us about anything unusual, such as very high-capacity trucks or specialised attachments, and we will confirm eligibility before quoting.

What the inspection covers

  • Identification: make, model, serial, hour meter, data plate and capacity information, attachment plates
  • Mast, carriage, forks and chains: wear, damage, deformation, security of fastenings
  • Hydraulic system: cylinders, hoses and fittings for leaks, damage and function
  • Steering, brakes, tyres or wheels, and drive and travel controls
  • Lift, tilt and auxiliary controls, including attachment function
  • Safety devices: horn, lights, alarms, seat belt or restraint, overhead guard and load backrest
  • Battery or fuel system condition and securing, as fitted
  • Visible structural condition of the chassis, counterweight and overhead guard

The inspection is visual and functional. Where a finding needs more than that, for example a measurement or a specialist test, we tell you so and explain the options.

What is not included

  • Operator training, evaluation or licensing
  • Repairs or adjustments. Any corrective work needs your separate authorisation
  • Daily pre-use checks, which remain the operator's responsibility
  • Load testing, non-destructive testing or engineering assessment unless specifically agreed in the scope
  • Trucks that are not safe to operate or cannot be accessed on the day

Where we do the work

At your site. We need the trucks available and accessible, a safe area to operate them for functional checks, and a site contact who can authorise operation.

    • Trucks charged or fuelled and with keys available
    • A clear area to drive and lift for functional checks
    • Site access and any induction arranged in advance

    How to prepare

    • Send a list of units with make, model and serial number if you have it. A photo of each data plate is enough
    • Note any attachments fitted and any units that are out of service
    • Gather previous inspection or repair records if available
    • Make sure data plates are legible and the units are clean enough to inspect
    • Tell us about site rules, safety induction and parking for our vehicle

    Records we ask for

    • Data plate and attachment plate details (photos are fine)
    • Previous inspection records, if any
    • Recent repair or modification records, if any

    Missing records do not stop an inspection. They help us confirm the scope and compare condition over time.

    What happens during the visit

    1. We check in with your site contact and confirm the list of units and the agreed scope
    2. Each unit is identified and its records are matched to the physical truck
    3. A documented walkaround records observed condition, including items found serviceable, with photographs
    4. Functional checks are carried out in a safe area
    5. We review findings with your site contact before leaving so nothing is a surprise

    What you receive

    • A record for each unit: identification, agreed scope, findings with photographs, disposition and recommended next actions
    • A fleet summary when more than one unit is inspected, showing status at a glance
    • Clear separation between items that need attention now and items to monitor

    Record format and sign-off follow the approved inspection procedure for this equipment.

    What affects the price

    • Number of units at the site
    • Truck types and attachments
    • Site location and travel
    • Access, scheduling and any site induction time
    • Whether previous records exist for comparison

    If we find a defect

    Findings are categorised so you know what must be addressed before the unit returns to service and what can be scheduled. Corrective work is a separate decision. You choose whether LiftRight, a partner or your own contractor carries it out, and we document the reinspection and final record afterwards.

    • Findings and their disposition are documented in the unit record
    • A repair quotation, if you want one, is always separate from the inspection findings
    • Reinspection of the corrected items closes the record

    Common questions

    Is a forklift inspection the same as forklift certification?

    No. People often say 'certification' to mean operator training. We inspect the equipment and document its condition. Operator training and evaluation are separate requirements that we do not provide.

    How often should a forklift be inspected?

    Operators carry out pre-use checks every shift. A periodic inspection by a competent person is generally expected at least annually, with manufacturer requirements and your own risk assessment setting the interval. We confirm the right interval for your equipment when we scope the work.

    Can you inspect a mixed fleet in one visit?

    Yes. Give us the list of units, including different truck types, and we coordinate the visit so your operation is disrupted as little as possible.

    What if a truck fails?

    We document the finding, explain what it means and set out the next step. Corrective work is authorised separately, and we reinspect once it is done.

    Do you need the truck out of service for the whole day?

    No. Each unit is typically out of service only for its own inspection. We schedule around your shifts where we can.
