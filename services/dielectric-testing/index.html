Dielectric testing for bucket trucks and aerial devices

Electrical testing of the insulating components on your aerial device, such as the upper boom, bucket liner and jib, to confirm they still perform as intended. Recorded separately from the structural and mechanical inspection.

Dielectric testing tells you about insulation performance. It does not replace the structural and mechanical inspection, and a passed test does not mean the device has no other defects.

Plain terms

Insulating section
The fibreglass or similar part of the boom designed to isolate the platform from the lower structure.
Liner
The removable insulating insert inside the bucket.

Equipment we test

  • Insulated bucket trucks and vehicle-mounted aerial devices
  • Bucket liners
  • Insulated jibs and tools, by arrangement

Tell us the device's insulation rating and category. We confirm eligibility before quoting.

What the test covers

  • Identification of the device and the specific components tested
  • Pre-test cleaning and visual check of insulating sections
  • Electrical testing of the agreed components to the agreed procedure
  • Recording of test parameters and results

The exact method, test values and acceptance criteria follow the approved procedure for the device's rating.

What is not included

  • Structural and mechanical inspection, unless booked together
  • Repairs, re-coating or liner replacement
  • Testing of components not agreed in the scope

Where we do the work

At your yard or site, subject to a suitable set-up area and dry conditions. Weather and humidity affect testing, so we agree a window rather than a fixed hour.

    • Dry conditions and a level set-up area
    • Clean insulating sections
    • Unit fuelled and ready

    How to prepare

    • Clean the boom and liner thoroughly
    • Send the device's rating and category and any previous test records
    • Plan for the unit to be unavailable for the test window

    Records we ask for

    • Rating plate and manufacturer data
    • Previous dielectric test records, if any
    • Records of repairs to insulating sections, if any

    What happens during the visit

    1. We confirm the device and components to be tested
    2. Insulating sections are checked visually and cleaned if needed
    3. The test is performed to the agreed procedure
    4. Results are recorded and reviewed with your site contact

    What you receive

    • A test record for each device stating the components tested, method, parameters, results and disposition
    • Clear next steps if a component does not meet the acceptance criteria

    What affects the price

    • Number of devices and components
    • Whether booked with a structural inspection
    • Site location and travel
    • Set-up and weather window

    If a component fails

    We record the result and explain what it means. Remedial work, such as liner replacement or boom refurbishment, is a separate decision that you authorise. Retesting after remedial work closes the record.

    • Result and disposition recorded per component
    • Remedial quotations, if requested, are separate from results
    • Retest closes the record

    Common questions

    How often is dielectric testing needed?

    Intervals depend on the device's rating, use and the applicable requirements. We confirm the interval that applies to your equipment when we scope the work.

    Can you test on a rainy day?

    No. Moisture affects results. We agree a weather window and reschedule if conditions are unsuitable.
