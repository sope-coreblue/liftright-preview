We keep the different inspection scopes distinct. An annual inspection, a structural inspection and a pre-start check are not the same thing, and your record says exactly which one was done.

Plain terms

MEWP
Mobile Elevating Work Platform. The umbrella term for scissor lifts, boom lifts and similar machines that lift people to work at height.
Scissor lift
A platform that rises vertically on folding supports. Slab (indoor) and rough-terrain types exist.
Boom lift
A platform on an articulating or telescopic arm that can reach up and out. Sometimes called a cherry picker.
Annual inspection
A periodic inspection of the whole machine by a competent person, documented in a record.
Structural inspection
A deeper look at load-bearing parts such as the chassis, scissor stack or boom sections, welds and pins. Required in some situations, for example after a set service life or an incident, and scoped separately.

Equipment we inspect

  • Slab and rough-terrain scissor lifts
  • Articulating and telescopic boom lifts
  • Vertical mast lifts and personnel lifts
  • Trailer-mounted boom lifts

Tell us the make, model and platform height. We confirm eligibility for very large or unusual machines before quoting.

What the inspection covers

  • Identification: make, model, serial, hour meter, data and capacity plates, decals and manuals present
  • Chassis, outriggers or stabilisers, wheels and tyres
  • Scissor stack or boom sections, pins, slide pads and visible welds for wear, damage or deformation
  • Platform, guardrails, gates, anchorage points and floor
  • Hydraulic system: cylinders, hoses, fittings and holding function
  • Controls at platform and ground, emergency lowering, emergency stop and interlocks
  • Tilt, load-sensing and other safety systems as fitted, checked for function
  • Battery, charger or engine condition and securing, as fitted

Structural inspection, where required, is scoped and quoted separately so you know exactly what is being done and why.

What is not included

  • Operator training or familiarisation
  • Repairs or adjustments. Corrective work needs your separate authorisation
  • Daily pre-start checks, which remain the operator's responsibility
  • Load testing or non-destructive testing unless specifically agreed in the scope
  • Machines that cannot be safely operated or accessed on the day

Where we do the work

At your site, yard or project. We need firm, level ground with enough clear height to raise the platform, the machine available and charged or fuelled, and a site contact who can authorise operation.

    • Clear, level area with overhead clearance to elevate
    • Units charged or fuelled with keys available
    • Site access and induction arranged in advance

    How to prepare

    • Send a list of units with make, model, serial and platform height. Data plate photos are enough
    • Tell us about any incidents, repairs or long-stored units
    • Gather previous inspection records if available
    • Clear a level area where each unit can be fully elevated
    • Confirm site rules, induction and where we can park

    Records we ask for

    • Data plate details and manufacturer information
    • Previous annual or structural inspection records, if any
    • Repair, modification or incident records, if any

    What happens during the visit

    1. We confirm the unit list and the scope with your site contact
    2. Each unit is identified and matched to its records
    3. A documented walkaround records condition, including serviceable items, with photographs
    4. Functional checks of controls, safety systems and emergency functions are carried out in a safe area
    5. Findings are reviewed with your site contact before we leave

    What you receive

    • A record for each unit stating the scope performed, identification, findings with photographs, disposition and next actions
    • A fleet summary for multi-unit visits
    • Clear notes on any item that needs a separate structural scope or specialist test

    What affects the price

    • Number and type of units
    • Scope: annual, structural or both
    • Platform height and machine size
    • Site location and travel
    • Access and scheduling

    If we find a defect

    We document the finding, explain its significance and state whether the machine should be taken out of service. Corrective work is a separate decision that you authorise, whether LiftRight, a partner or your own contractor does it. We reinspect the corrected items and close the record.

    • Disposition is recorded per unit
    • Repair quotations, if requested, are kept separate from findings
    • Reinspection closes the record

    Common questions

    What is the difference between an annual inspection and a structural inspection?

    An annual inspection covers the whole machine's condition and function. A structural inspection focuses on load-bearing parts and is required in particular circumstances, such as after a defined service life or an incident. We tell you which applies and scope them separately.

    Do you inspect rental machines on a job site?

    We can, with the owner's agreement. Tell us who owns the machine so the record goes to the right place.

    Can you inspect scissor lifts and boom lifts in the same visit?

    Yes. Mixed MEWP fleets are common and we coordinate them in one visit where the site allows.

    What if there is not enough room to fully elevate a unit?

    Tell us in advance. We can often move the unit to a suitable area on site, or agree a different arrangement before the visit.
