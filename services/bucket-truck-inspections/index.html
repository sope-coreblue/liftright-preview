Bucket truck and vehicle-mounted aerial device inspections

Structural and mechanical inspections of bucket trucks and other vehicle-mounted aerial devices, carried out at your yard or site with a documented record for each unit. Dielectric testing of insulating components is offered as a distinct additional service.

Structural and mechanical inspection and dielectric testing answer different questions. One is about the condition and function of the device. The other is about the electrical insulating performance of specific components. Your record states which was performed.

Equipment we inspect

  • Insulated and non-insulated bucket trucks
  • Telescopic and articulating vehicle-mounted aerial devices
  • Material-handling aerial devices and jibs where fitted
  • Platform-equipped digger derricks, by arrangement

Tell us the manufacturer, model and insulation rating if any. We confirm eligibility before quoting.

What the structural and mechanical inspection covers

  • Identification: manufacturer, model, serial, chassis identification, data and rating plates, decals
  • Pedestal, turret, rotation bearing and mounting to the chassis
  • Boom sections, pins, visible welds and fibreglass or insulating sections for damage, contamination or deterioration
  • Platform or bucket, liner, mounting and leveling system
  • Outriggers, stabilisers and interlocks
  • Hydraulic system: cylinders, hoses, fittings, holding valves and leaks
  • Controls at platform and lower station, emergency stop and emergency lowering
  • Visible condition of the chassis mounting, subframe and attachment points

Dielectric testing is not part of this scope. It is quoted and recorded separately.

What is not included

  • Dielectric testing, unless added as a separate service
  • Repairs or adjustments. Corrective work needs your separate authorisation
  • Chassis, brake or commercial vehicle safety inspections
  • Operator training
  • Load testing or non-destructive testing unless specifically agreed in the scope

Preparing for the visit

Where the work happens

At your yard or site. We need firm, level ground to set up the unit, enough clear space and height to extend the boom through its range, and a site contact who can authorise operation.

  • Level set-up area with overhead clearance
  • Unit fuelled and ready with keys available
  • Access and any site induction arranged

How to prepare

  • Send the manufacturer, model and serial of each device, and the chassis unit number
  • Tell us whether the device is insulated and the rating if known
  • Gather previous inspection and dielectric test records if available
  • Clean the boom and bucket so insulating sections can be inspected
  • Confirm set-up space, site rules and induction

Records we ask for

  • Rating plate and manufacturer data
  • Previous structural inspection and dielectric test records, if any
  • Repair, modification or incident records, if any

Records and follow-up

During the visit

  1. We confirm the unit list and scope with your site contact
  2. Each device is identified and matched to its records
  3. A documented walkaround records condition, including serviceable items, with photographs
  4. Functional checks of the boom, controls, interlocks and emergency functions are carried out in a safe set-up area
  5. Findings are reviewed with your site contact before we leave

What you receive

  • A record for each device stating the scope performed, identification, findings with photographs, disposition and next actions
  • A separate dielectric test record when that service is performed
  • A fleet summary for multi-unit visits

If we find a defect

We document the finding, explain its significance and state whether the device should be taken out of service. Corrective work is authorised separately, by you, whether LiftRight, a partner or your own contractor carries it out. Reinspection or retesting of the corrected items closes the record.

  • Disposition recorded per device
  • Repair quotations, if requested, are separate from findings
  • Reinspection or retest closes the record

Pricing and questions

What affects the price

  • Number and type of devices
  • Scope: structural and mechanical, dielectric testing, or both
  • Site location and travel
  • Set-up space and scheduling
  • Whether previous records exist

Questions we are asked

Does a structural inspection include dielectric testing?

No. They are different services with different equipment and procedures. We offer dielectric testing separately and the record states which service was performed.

Can you inspect the truck chassis as well?

Our inspection covers the aerial device and its mounting to the chassis. Commercial vehicle safety inspections of the chassis itself are a separate requirement.

How should I prepare an insulated boom for inspection?

Clean it. Contamination on insulating sections hides damage and affects testing. Let us know about any recent repairs to fibreglass sections.

What happens if the device fails?

We document the finding and the disposition, explain what it means for the unit's use and set out the next step. Corrective work is a separate decision, and we reinspect once it is done.

Terms used on this page

Vehicle-mounted aerial device
A boom and platform or bucket mounted on a truck chassis. Bucket trucks, digger derricks with platforms and similar units.
Structural and mechanical inspection
Inspection of the boom, pedestal, turret, outriggers, platform, hydraulics and controls for condition and function.
Dielectric testing
Electrical testing of insulating sections such as the upper boom, bucket liner or jib to confirm they still insulate as intended. A separate service with its own equipment and procedure.
